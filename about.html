About the project

AgriSmart Rwanda is a student data-intelligence prototype that organizes published agricultural statistics into accessible dashboards. It helps users explore crop production, compare reported crop yields across districts, and review district-level agricultural practices and soil-erosion indicators.

The goal is to make official data easier to explore and to identify questions that may deserve further investigation—not to replace field research or professional agricultural advice.

1. The problem

Agricultural statistics can be difficult to compare when information is spread across multiple tables. Students, researchers, planners, and other interested users may need a simpler way to inspect crop indicators and district-level differences.

AgriSmart Rwanda brings selected published indicators together in one website with charts, filters, district comparisons, and CSV downloads.

2. Project objectives

Explore national crop data

Display reported crop production, yield, cultivated area, and harvested area.

Compare districts

Allow users to select a crop and compare its reported yield across districts.

Review other indicators

Explore reported agricultural practices and plot erosion classifications by district.

Support further investigation

Highlight descriptive differences and make selected data available for additional analysis.

4. Methodology

  1. Source selection: Select relevant published NISR tables for national crops, district yields, agricultural practices, and erosion classifications.
  2. Data preparation: Convert selected tables into structured CSV files for use by the website.
  3. Validation: Check expected columns and filter records for the relevant year and season where applicable. Missing values are treated as unavailable rather than automatically treated as zero.
  4. Descriptive analysis: Calculate simple summaries and rankings from available reported values, and compare selected districts using the same indicator.
  5. Visualization: Display results using charts, summary indicators, comparison tables, and downloadable CSV files.
  6. Interpretation: Present results as descriptions of the published data and encourage users to check source definitions before drawing conclusions.

The website is a descriptive analytics prototype. It does not currently train a machine-learning model or make farm-level predictions.

6. Limitations and responsible interpretation

  • The dashboard depends on the coverage, quality, definitions, and reporting choices of the published source tables.
  • District-level aggregates do not describe every farmer, farm, or individual plot.
  • A difference between districts does not establish its cause. The dashboards cannot prove that a particular practice caused a yield or erosion outcome.
  • Some crop categories may overlap; totals across categories should not be added unless the source confirms that they are mutually exclusive.
  • Zero or missing yield values may need verification against the original table before interpretation.
  • The current version is a prototype for exploration and learning, not a replacement for field assessment, official advice, or a full agricultural decision-support system.
Interpretation rule: Treat a highlighted district indicator as a starting point for a question. Verify the source definition and consult local evidence before recommending an intervention.

7. Expected benefits

  • Make selected official agricultural indicators easier to explore.
  • Help students practice data preparation, analysis, visualization, and responsible interpretation.
  • Support preliminary comparisons that can guide follow-up research and questions.
  • Encourage the use of published evidence when discussing agricultural productivity in Rwanda.
